Please consider my research article **"Interested Expertise: Defence-Industrial Ties and the Western Policy Debate on the Russia–Ukraine Conflict"** for publication in *Russia in Global Affairs*.

The article asks through which institutional channels defence-industrial interests enter the Western, mainly American, policy debate on the Russia–Ukraine conflict, and how strong the public-record evidence is for each. Using a qualitative synthesis of Government Accountability Office data, think-tank funding studies, published investigations of commentators' affiliations, research on congressional portfolios and SIPRI data, it finds strong support for three channels (post-government employment, think-tank funding and undisclosed media affiliations) and inconclusive evidence for a fourth (legislators' personal investments). It argues that these channels bias the supply of expertise without proving corrupt intent. I believe the topic will interest your readers because it examines the political economy of Western expert debate, a dimension of the conflict that has received less systematic attention than strategy or alliance politics.
