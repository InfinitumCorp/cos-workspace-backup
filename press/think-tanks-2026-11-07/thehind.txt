A proposal for an 800 to 1,500-word Security analysis: strategic autonomy and the mirror trap. The argument is that copying a rival's playbook in intelligence, information warfare and cyber feels like keeping pace but in fact ratchets escalation, and that India's tradition of strategic autonomy is a real advantage here. I would engage the main counterargument, that refusing to mirror cedes the initiative. It builds on a framework from my recent paper.

The paper is "Can't Beat Them, Join Them?: The Adversarial Mirror Ratchet™" (Infinitum Corporation® Military Doctrine Series, November 2026; 26 pages, about 6,500 words; PDF attached). It sets out the Adversarial Mirror Ratchet™, a term I coined for how rivals in a long conflict copy each other's methods in terror, intelligence, information operations and cyber, and how each copy ratchets escalation upward instead of settling the contest. It is strategic analysis from public sources, not instruction, and it takes no side.
