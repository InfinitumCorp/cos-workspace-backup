**Subject / headline:** Pitch: Friendly fire. A cyberattack on a Polish supermarket chain and the strain inside the Poland-Ukraine alliance

---

(For UnHerd's contact form. Pitch, ~1,500-word essay.)

On 26 July 2026, a group calling itself the Azov Cyber Legion encrypted servers at Lewiatan, one of Poland's largest retail chains, and knocked payment systems offline at about ten outlets. Its demands were political, not financial. Lewiatan refused to engage, restored from backups and treated it as a crime.

The essay would use the attack as a way into a relationship Western commentary treats as unbreakable: the alliance between Poland and Ukraine. It would cover the unresolved dispute over the 1943 Volhynia massacres, which Poland's parliament has recognised as genocide, the grain-import rows, Poland's role as the logistics hub for Western aid and the diplomatic tension after a Ukrainian unit was renamed in July 2026. It would end with what has worked to ease such disputes elsewhere: joint historical commissions and school exchanges. The cyber lesson for European retailers, tested offline backups and segmented payment systems, would run through it.
