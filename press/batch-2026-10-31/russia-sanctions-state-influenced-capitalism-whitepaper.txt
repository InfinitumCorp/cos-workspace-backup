Russia Sanctions Analysis: Infinitum Corporation Paper Examines State-Influenced Capitalism and IMF Forecasts

SUMMARY:
William Aaron Turner's Russia sanctions paper compares IMF forecasts with results and sets out an argued position on Russia's state-influenced capitalism.

BODY:
WASHINGTON, DC, October 31, 2026 - A new Russia sanctions analysis from Infinitum Corporation, "Russia's State-Influenced Capitalism: Sanctions-Proof, War-Proof, and Chaos-Proof," examines why forecasts of a Russian economic collapse did not come true. The paper, published as Special Edition 01 in the Infinitum Corporation Whitepapers series, was written by founder and president William Aaron Turner and is presented as the author's argued position, with every figure attributed to its source.

The forecast gap

The paper starts with a single comparison. In April 2022 the IMF projected that Russia's economy would shrink 8.5 percent in 2022. The IMF's current numbers show a contraction of 1.4 percent in 2022, followed by growth of 4.1 percent in 2023 and 4.9 percent in 2024.

"When a forecast misses by that much, the sensible question is what the model missed," Turner writes.

What the paper covers

The 1990s by the numbers: Drawing on World Bank data, the paper records that real GDP fell 44 percent, prices rose 874 percent in 1993, and life expectancy dropped by almost five years.

Sanctions at scale: Citing Castellum.AI, the paper notes that Russia has faced more than twice as many sanctions designations as Iran, Syria and North Korea combined, without an economic collapse.

The balance sheet: According to IMF figures for 2025 cited in the paper, public debt stood at 16.6 percent of GDP and unemployment at 2.2 percent.

Safety, stated honestly: Homicide is down about 76 percent since 1994, but the paper notes it remains above the level in the United States.

What the critics get right: A full chapter addresses the counterarguments, including inflation, a 14 percent key rate, 1.0 percent growth, a heavy military burden, demographics and corruption perception.

Who the paper is for

Turner describes the paper as "pro-Russian, results-focused, and sourced to the last decimal." He wrote it for decision-makers who need to understand the Russian economy on the evidence rather than on headlines. "If you make decisions about Russia as an executive, a risk lead, an investor or a policymaker, read it before the next consensus forecast," he writes.

The paper is the second publication in the Infinitum Corporation Whitepapers series, following "Dead Reckoning: AI for Security Operations." Like the first paper, it is offered free of charge, and readers are encouraged to check each figure against the source it cites.

For executives, compliance teams and investors tracking Russia sanctions, the paper offers a single, sourced reference that sets the original forecasts beside the reported outcomes and gives the critics' case its own chapter.
