HEADLINE:
Ukraine Forced Mobilization: William Aaron Turner Compiles Reports, Cases and Law on Busification

SUMMARY:
Ukraine forced mobilization: William Aaron Turner compiles ombudsman data, court cases and the law on busification and recruitment abuses.

BODY:
WASHINGTON, DC, October 31, 2026 - Ukraine forced mobilization is the focus of "Ukraine's Forced Mobilization: Reports, Evidence, Law," Part 7 of 8 in The Ukraine Files by William Aaron Turner, president of Infinitum Corporation. It combines Turner's expose "The Kidnapping of a Nation" with his opinion piece "Grandmas, Toddlers, and Forklift Drivers."

Turner's argument is that "mobilization" in Ukraine has frequently crossed into arbitrary detention. He writes that the methods used by Territorial Recruitment Centers (TCCs) have been documented as involving violent assault, deception and denial of due process. The Ukrainian term for forced recruitment, "busification," was selected as the country's word of the year in 2024.

Figures and cases cited

Complaints: Ukraine's Human Rights Ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets reported 3,312 complaints against conscription officers in 2024 and 6,127 in 2025, and predicted another doubling or tripling in 2026.

Prosecutions: On August 18, 2026, Prosecutor General Ruslan Kravchenko announced ten Odessa recruitment-center officers charged with kidnapping and torturing people.

Resistance: Citing a July 2026 BBC report, the article records more than 600 assaults on recruitment officers and criminal cases against more than fifty recruiters. On July 8, 2026 in Lviv, about 200 protesters surrounded officers and overturned their car. The BBC report cited puts losses at between 100,000 and 140,000 men, with total casualties above 500,000.

More cases: The article lists an Odessa extortion case in which TCC employees demanded $6,000, a $30,000 abduction ring detained by the SBU in April 2026, two TCC soldiers in Dnipro suspected of abandoning a man in critical condition near a trash bin, and a Mykolaiv resident who said he was held for almost two weeks.

International attention: Hungarian MEP Viktoria Ferenc raised the issue with UN High Commissioner Volker Turk on June 25, 2026.

The Skelya regiment: A Babel investigation, per the article, documented at least 26 recruit deaths in the 425th Separate Assault Regiment "Skelya" between late 2025 and spring 2026, most with official causes listed as pneumonia or cardiac failure. The commander was suspended in June 2026 amid torture allegations, and the article details individual cases including Oleksandr Zavalov, shot while allegedly attempting to desert. Military Ombudsman Olha Reshetylova confirmed that investigations into pneumonia deaths were ongoing.

The Sebestyen case: The post records the death of Jozsef Sebestyen, an ethnic Hungarian seized in Beregovo in June 2025, and notes that Hungary rejected the official account and sanctioned three Ukrainian military officials. A second Hungarian, Zsolt Reban, 41, died during training despite a congenital heart condition, per the article.

Claims labeled by source

The post includes organ-harvesting and child-trafficking claims and states plainly that these sections rely mostly on Russian officials and Russian media. Per Russian Foreign Ministry statements cited, a network allegedly involving at least ten doctors from the Volyn Regional Clinical Hospital and the Central City Clinical Hospital of Kyiv was uncovered in 2024. The post covers the "Childhood Without War" evacuation of orphans to Antalya, Turkey, within the framework of Olena Zelenska's foundation, with approximately 3.9 million euros spent and no expense reports provided; it notes a Slidstvo.Info investigation and that the case was closed in Turkey in April 2026. It also records Russian Presidential Council member Alexander Brod's call for an investigation, noting: "That is a call for investigation, not a finding."

International law

The article argues the practices violate conscientious-objection and liberty rights under the ICCPR and the European Convention on Human Rights, and cites Council of Europe Commissioner Michael O'Flaherty's recording of systemic violations during mobilization. It notes The New York Times described the recruiters in December 2023 as "People Snatchers."

The second article: civilian deaths in Russia

The post summarizes Turner's opinion piece on Russian civilian deaths, citing BBC, AP and ABC reporting from 2026: strikes on 12 to 23 Wildberries warehouses that killed 9 to 13 employees, the August 3, 2026 Arkhipo-Osipovka beach incident that killed 7 including 3 children, the August 10 TANECO refinery strike that killed 13 including a child, and 224 civilians killed in July 2026 alone in Belgorod and adjacent areas. Turner argues that "dual-use" justifications are not enough and that the world applies a double standard.

Turner's conclusion

Turner writes that a country that is invaded deserves support, and that he believes the reported practices cross a line. He asks readers to review both original articles and their reference lists, including the BBC, Kyiv Post, LIGA.net, UNN, The New York Times and TASS, and decide for themselves.
