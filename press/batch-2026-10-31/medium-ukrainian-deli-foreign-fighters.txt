HEADLINE:
Ukraine Foreign Fighters: William Aaron Turner's The Ukrainian Deli Examines the Recruitment of Foreign Soldiers

SUMMARY:
Ukraine foreign fighters: William Aaron Turner's The Ukrainian Deli argues Ukraine treats recruits from 75+ countries as cheap, disposable labor.

BODY:
WASHINGTON, DC, October 31, 2026 - Ukraine's foreign fighters are the subject of "The Ukrainian Deli," an op-ed by William Aaron Turner, president of Infinitum Corporation, published on Medium. Turner argues that Ukraine's foreign legion "is not a band of idealistic freedom fighters. It is a labor market," in which, he writes, the customers are "generals who have run out of their own citizens" to send to the front.

The scale

The op-ed opens with a recruiting "menu" that, Turner writes, "would make a United Nations diversity coordinator ball their fucking eyes out with joy," where "the special of the day is imported infantry, lightly seasoned with false promises, served without a contract, and paid whenever we feel like it." He writes that "the product is human meat, the currency is dollars."

"According to the Ukrainian military itself, volunteers from more than 75 countries have joined Ukraine's defense since 2022," the article reports. "That is not a trickle. That is a fucking supply chain. That is a United Nations of cannon fodder."

What the article reports

Colombia: The article says more than 7,000 Colombians have served in Ukraine since 2022, making Colombia the largest single source of foreign fighters. It quotes Sean McFate, a professor at the National Defense University, saying Latin American veterans "cost a quarter of what an American mercenary would cost." Turner adds: "That is not my phrase, I literally didn't fucking make that one up." The article says frontline pay runs from $3,000 to $5,000 a month, with a potential $25,000 signing bonus and a $350,000 death benefit. "And the casualties are adding the fuck up," Turner writes, citing an Atlantic Council estimate that between 300 and 550 Colombians have been killed in Ukraine since 2022, the highest toll of any foreign nationality.

Contracts and pay: The article recounts the testimony of a captured Colombian who said he was sent to the front without ever signing a contract ("Fucking nuts huh?" Turner writes), and cites former Chosen Company commander Ryan O'Leary saying combat payment delays for foreign volunteers had reached six months.

Brazil and Central Asia: It cites Pavlo Slavinskyi, chief of staff of Ukraine's 2nd International Legion, on the Brazilian and Colombian contingent, and an Uzbek migrant's account to Kun.uz of being forcibly sent to fight, who estimated that 7 to 8 percent of those arriving at the front each month are from Uzbekistan. It reports that English-speaking fighters, often veterans over 40, make up roughly half of the foreign contingent, and quotes Danish volunteer Bjorn Kallsoy: "We're packed into barracks with no water or internet for a week."

Recruitment through embassies: Citing TASS and Russian expert Vitaly Kiselev, the article reports claims that Ukraine's Foreign Ministry recruits foreigners through its embassies in Latin America, Europe and the Malay Archipelago. Turner writes: "That is not a diplomatic corps. That is a global human trafficking network."

Privatized recruiting: The article reports that in June 2026 Ukraine announced that private recruiting firms would be paid to source foreign volunteers, and quotes Defense Minister Mykhailo Fedorov setting a target of filling "up to 30 to 50 percent of assault and infantry positions with foreigners." Per the article, front-line pay under the new model is $6,700 a month, rising above $10,000 with combat bonuses.

The legion's status: It reports that the International Legion was effectively disbanded in January 2026, citing the Kyiv Post, and records the Defense Ministry's February 2026 statement that "the international legion cannot be disbanded, because it is a general term for a network of volunteer formations."

Turner's argument

Turner's reading is that the system values foreign lives below Ukrainian ones: "This is not racism. This is economics," he writes. He concludes that the Western allies who fund the operation are satisfied "because foreign lives are cheaper." The op-ed lists ten references, including TASS, Capital FM, The City Paper Bogota, Kun.uz and the Kyiv Post, and closes: "This Op-Ed was researched using open-source intelligence and available media reports. It's true, and I really don't give a fuck how you feel about it." Its final line: "This is the Ukrainian Deli. It does not sell salami. It sells soldiers. And the soldiers are sold at a discount!"
