HEADLINE:
Eastern Economic Forum 2026: William Aaron Turner Examines 80-Nation Turnout and Russia's Asian Trade

SUMMARY:
Eastern Economic Forum 2026: William Aaron Turner reviews the 80-country Vladivostok turnout, deal values and Russia's trade with China, India and ASEAN.

BODY:
WASHINGTON, DC, October 31, 2026 - The Eastern Economic Forum 2026 in Vladivostok is the subject of a new analysis by William Aaron Turner, founder and president of Infinitum Corporation. Titled "Eastern Economic Forum 2026: Is Russia Isolated?", the piece is Part 3 of 8 in The Ukraine Files series and uses the 11th Eastern Economic Forum (EEF) to test a simple question: what does the evidence say about Russia's international isolation?

In September 2026, delegations from 80 countries filled the halls of the Far Eastern Federal University in Vladivostok. The post walks through the forum's numbers, statements from Western officials and institutions, and the trade and energy links between Russia and Asia.

Forum figures cited in the analysis

Attendance: More than 5,000 representatives from 80 countries attended the four-day 11th EEF, up from 75 countries the year before. Deputy Prime Minister Yury Trutnev is quoted: "I would struggle to name a major country not present at this forum."

Agreements: At the 10th EEF in 2025, organizers reported 358 agreements worth 6.058 trillion rubles ($71 billion). The 2026 forum was expected to sign 380 agreements worth 6.5 trillion rubles ($79.9 billion).

Regional investment: By 2026 the cumulative figures were 6.8 trillion rubles in investments, 1,118 new enterprises and 163,000 new jobs in the Far East.

Russia's trade with Asia

China: China-Russia trade totaled $228.1 billion in 2025, down 6.9 percent from the 2024 record, before turning up again in 2026, up 26.3 percent over seven months. The article also quotes the Financial Times saying Moscow is "more dependent on Beijing" than vice versa.

India: India-Russia goods trade rose from about $13 billion in 2021-22 to nearly $60 billion in 2025-26, a relationship the post describes as lopsided toward Indian imports, mostly oil.

Southeast Asia: ASEAN's two-way merchandise trade with Russia reached $17.8 billion in 2025, a figure ASEAN Secretary-General Kao Kim Hourn called "still very much modest."

Payments: According to the article, more than 99 percent of commercial payments between Russia and China are now made in rubles and yuan.

Energy, infrastructure and currencies

The article reports that Power of Siberia 1 reached its designed 38 billion cubic meters a year in 2025, that Russia and China signed a memorandum on Power of Siberia 2 (while Western outlets reported no final deal in May 2026), and that the 531-kilometer Pacific Railway opened in September 2025. It cites the yuan's 54 percent share of Moscow Exchange trading in May 2026, and data that only about 5 percent of Russian oil exports are now settled in dollars, down from 55 percent before 2022.

The military and security dimension

Turner argues Russia's defense ties with Asia have expanded since 2022, citing the first ASEAN-Russia naval exercise (ARNEX) in December 2025, the "Orruda 2026" Russia-Indonesia drill, "Typhoon-2026" and "Laros-2026" with Laos, and Russia's co-chair role on the ADMM Plus humanitarian demining working group for 2024-2027.

Both sides of the sanctions debate

The analysis quotes U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent ("If you're going to do something 19 times, you've failed"), Secretary of State Marco Rubio saying America was "running out of things to sanction," and J.D. Vance: "There is a fantasy that if we just give more money, more weapons, or more sanctions, victory is at hand." It cites the Institute of International Finance, which concluded in July 2025 that sanctions "have fallen short of fully isolating Russia," and Chatham House's "Fortress Russia" analysis, noting a record 26,665 sanctions on Russia against 6,027 on Iran. It also presents the other side of the Chatham House research it relies on, including an estimated cumulative dollar GDP loss "well in excess of $1.6 trillion." Turner argues those costs must be weighed against the predictions of collapse made in 2022.

His conclusion is stated as his own reading of the data: "The isolation story was a narrative, not a result." The article's closing line: "The isolation cage was built, but the door was left wide open." He invites readers to review the full reference list, which includes Chatham House, the IIF, Bloomberg, Reuters, TASS and Nikkei Asia, and decide for themselves.
