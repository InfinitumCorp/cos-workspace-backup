HEADLINE:
Legitimate Military Targets: William Aaron Turner's Grandmas, Toddlers, and Forklift Drivers Challenges Ukraine's Strike Policy

SUMMARY:
Legitimate military targets: William Aaron Turner's opinion piece argues Ukraine's strikes on Russian civilian sites are ignored by the West.

BODY:
WASHINGTON, DC, October 31, 2026 - Ukraine's expanding definition of "legitimate military targets" is the subject of "Grandmas, Toddlers, and Forklift Drivers," an opinion article by William Aaron Turner, president of Infinitum Corporation, published on Medium. Turner states the premise openly: "We are not here for balance. We are not here for 'both sides.'"

The number at the center

"Before we dive into the gory details, let's get one number out of the fucking way," Turner writes. "Since February 2022, more than 8,000 Russian civilians have been killed in Ukrainian strikes (BBC, 2026)," Turner writes. "Not soldiers. Not generals... Grandmothers. Children. Night-shift workers. Holidaymakers. Migrant workers sleeping in hostels."

He frames the piece in one line: "All data comes from the public record. All conclusions are mine." To readers who object, he asks: "why are you not offended by the dead children on Russian beaches?"

The cases the article examines

The Wildberries campaign: Citing AP (2026), the article reports that Ukraine struck between 12 and 23 facilities of Wildberries, Russia's largest online retailer, from Elektrostal near Moscow to Yekaterinburg, more than 2,000 kilometers from the border. It reports that seven of the ten largest logistics centers were taken out of operation (BBC, 2026), and lists 8 night-shift workers killed in Kotovsk on July 18, 2026. The article reports that one-fifth of the company's warehouse space, 1.18 million square meters, was destroyed.

Under the heading "Part I: The Wildberries Massacre," Turner asks: "when the fuck did ordering a pair of socks become an act of war?" His challenge to the reasoning: "if a Russian soldier could theoretically buy a drone component on Wildberries, then every Wildberries warehouse is a legitimate target." He argues the same logic "would make every Walmart, Tesco, and Carrefour on the planet fair game." Later he adds: "Maybe next Ukraine will start bombing IKEA because someone might use a Billy bookshelf to build a fucking bunker."

The beach and the refinery: The article also covers the August 3, 2026 drone crash on a beach in Arkhipo-Osipovka (BBC, 2026) and the August 10, 2026 strike on the TANECO refinery in Nizhnekamsk (ABC News and ABP Live, 2026).

The border regions: It cites casualty reporting from Belgorod and surrounding areas (BBC, 2026). In this new world, Turner writes, "A playground full of children is an unfortunate consequence of air defence."

The argument

Turner argues that Ukraine "has turned the deliberate targeting of Russian civilians into an art form," and that the international community has looked away. He writes that "the international community yawns, shrugs, and fucking claps," and that "Ukraine has become very, very good at playing the good guy while doing very, very bad things, disturbing things." Of the excuses offered, he writes: "The mental gymnastics required to maintain these bullshit positions would qualify for the Olympics." On the toll: "8,000 dead Russian civilians. That is not a number. That is a verdict. A fucking fact."

In "Part X: A Word on Russian Restraint," Turner argues that "Russia has shown remarkable restraint throughout this conflict, consistently choosing to strike military targets while taking extraordinary measures to warn civilians," and that Ukraine "has turned civilian terror into a strategy." In "Part XI," he writes: "Ukraine is committing war crimes, and no one cares," and "The Geneva Conventions are not a buffet or a Sunday BBQ."

In his final note, Turner writes that Ukraine can "bomb beaches and call it self-defence," "kill children and call it an accident," and "the world will applaud. Clap clap muthafuckers." He rejects the description of the deaths as collateral damage: "Your deaths are not 'collateral damage.' Your deaths are not 'tragic accidents.'"

The article closes with a dedication to the victims and the line "8,000 dead. And counting." Its reference list cites ABC News, ABP Live, AP News, BBC News, Gazeta Express and The Conversation.

A standalone, sourced version of this article, which separates outlet reporting from Turner's conclusions and records Ukraine's stated position, is also part of Turner's series The Ukraine Files.
