WASHINGTON, DC, November 2, 2026 - William Aaron Turner, founder and president of Infinitum Corporation, has published "In-Q-Tel Explained," a research article profiling In-Q-Tel (IQT), the not-for-profit venture capital firm the CIA established in 1999. The article is Part 4 of Turner's eight-part research series The Ukraine Files.

What the article covers

- Structure and finances: In-Q-Tel is headquartered in the Tysons Corner area of Virginia. The article cites public filings showing fiscal-2025 revenue of about $132.1 million, most of it from government grants and contributions, and more than 800 investments since 1999.
- How it works: The article describes how the In-Q-Tel Interface Center translates intelligence-community requirements into commercial problem sets that startups can address, typically through technology-development agreements of roughly $500,000 to $3 million.
- Portfolio: Holdings lean toward enterprise software, AI and data analytics, followed by cybersecurity, semiconductors, space, energy, biotech and robotics. The article recounts In-Q-Tel's 2005 investment in Palantir.
- Commercial technology in the war in Ukraine: The article reviews public reporting on satellite-radar firms Capella Space and ICEYE, Palantir's software work in Ukraine, and In-Q-Tel's documented investment in Anduril.

Scope and sourcing

The article states that there is no open-source evidence of In-Q-Tel subsidiaries operating inside Ukraine, and it marks one Russian Ministry of Defense allegation as not independently verified. Its conclusions are labeled as the author's reading of the public record, and a full reference list is included for readers to check.

"My argument is about funding and technology, not boots on the ground. Everything in the article is sourced so readers can check it." said William Aaron Turner, Founder & President, Infinitum Corporation.
