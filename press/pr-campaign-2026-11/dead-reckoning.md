# Dead Reckoning Whitepaper: William Aaron Turner Gives Security Teams a Quarterly Check to Keep AI Tools Honest

Infinitum Corporation's Dead Reckoning whitepaper offers security operations teams a 90-day AI adoption plan, a 12-month roadmap and a recurring quarterly verification check.

WASHINGTON, DC, November 2, 2026 - Infinitum Corporation is highlighting a practical tool from its first whitepaper, "Dead Reckoning: AI for Security Operations in a Jammed, Spoofed, Watched World": a recurring quarterly check that lets security teams confirm their AI tools are still pointed in the right direction.

The paper, written by William Aaron Turner, Founder & President of Infinitum Corporation, borrows its method from navigation. When satellite signals fail, navigators fix their position from what they can verify: last known position, heading, speed, time and a second source. Turner argues that security operations centers adopting artificial intelligence need the same discipline, because a confident AI output is not the same as a correct one.

The paper sets out a sequence in three stages. A 90-day plan covers governance, a measured baseline and a pilot with analysts kept in the loop. A 12-month roadmap extends the tools that prove themselves. The quarterly dead reckoning check then compares results against the baseline and against an independent source for every critical signal.

The case for discipline rests on public data. Citing Verizon's 2026 Data Breach Investigations Report, the paper notes that exploiting vulnerabilities is now the top way into a breach, at 31 percent, up from 20 percent. IBM and the Ponemon Institute report that the mean time to identify and contain a breach rose to 247 days. IBM also found that organizations using security AI and automation extensively averaged USD 4.00 million per breach, against USD 5.93 million for those using none. Turner stresses that this is an association, not a guarantee.

The paper is written for security leaders, analysts and risk owners who must judge AI tools on evidence rather than vendor claims. It compares the defender's environment to one of jammed and spoofed signals and constant observation, and states plainly that this is a comparison, not a claim that any network is a front line.

Infinitum Corporation offers the paper free of charge, and readers are encouraged to check each figure against its cited source.
