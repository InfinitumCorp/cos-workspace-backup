# Ukraine's Foreign Fighters: William Aaron Turner's Op-Ed The Ukrainian Deli Argues Recruits Are Treated as a Labor Market

William Aaron Turner's op-ed examines the recruitment, pay and casualties of foreign volunteers in Ukraine, drawing on Ukrainian, Colombian, U.S. and Central Asian sources.

WASHINGTON, DC, November 2, 2026 - William Aaron Turner, Founder & President of Infinitum Corporation, has published "The Ukrainian Deli," an opinion article on Medium about the recruitment of foreign soldiers to fight for Ukraine.

Turner's central argument is that Ukraine's foreign legion functions less as a band of idealistic volunteers and more as a labor market, recruiting from abroad as domestic manpower runs short. The article is openly written as opinion, and its figures are attributed to the sources it cites.

The op-ed notes that, according to the Ukrainian military, volunteers from more than 75 countries have joined Ukraine's defense since 2022. It reports that more than 7,000 Colombians have served, making Colombia the largest single source, and cites an Atlantic Council estimate that between 300 and 550 Colombians have been killed. It quotes Sean McFate, a professor at the National Defense University, on why Latin American veterans are recruited, and reports frontline pay of $3,000 to $5,000 a month, with signing bonuses and death benefits.

The article also examines contracts and pay. It recounts the testimony of a captured Colombian who said he was sent to the front without signing a contract, and cites former Chosen Company commander Ryan O'Leary on payment delays of up to six months for foreign volunteers. It draws on remarks by Pavlo Slavinskyi of Ukraine's 2nd International Legion and on an Uzbek migrant's account to the outlet Kun.uz.

Turner argues that the recruits' own accounts of pay, contracts and losses deserve more attention from the governments and media of the countries they come from. Readers are encouraged to follow the cited sources and weigh the evidence themselves.
