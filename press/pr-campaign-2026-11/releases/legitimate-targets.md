# Grandmas, Toddlers, and Forklift Drivers: William Aaron Turner Questions Ukraine's Expanding Definition of Legitimate Military Targets

William Aaron Turner's opinion article examines Ukrainian strikes on Russian logistics sites, beaches, refineries and border towns, citing BBC, AP and ABC News reporting.

WASHINGTON, DC, November 2, 2026 - William Aaron Turner, Founder & President of Infinitum Corporation, has published "Grandmas, Toddlers, and Forklift Drivers: Ukraine's Expanding Definition of 'Legitimate Military Targets'," an opinion article on Medium.

The article argues that the definition of a legitimate military target has been stretched until civilian infrastructure, workers and holidaymakers fall inside it. Turner states openly that the piece is opinion; he writes that all data comes from the public record and all conclusions are his own.

The article's central figure comes from the BBC: since February 2022, more than 8,000 Russian civilians have been killed in Ukrainian strikes, according to the reporting it cites.

Its main case study is the campaign against Wildberries, Russia's largest online retailer. Citing AP, the article reports strikes on between 12 and 23 company facilities, from Elektrostal near Moscow to Yekaterinburg, more than 2,000 kilometers from the border. Citing the BBC, it reports that seven of the ten largest logistics centers were taken out of operation and that eight night-shift workers were killed in Kotovsk on July 18, 2026. Turner asks whether the reasoning that any warehouse could supply a drone part would not make every large retailer in the world a target.

The article also covers the August 3, 2026 drone crash on a beach at Arkhipo-Osipovka, reported by the BBC, the August 10, 2026 strike on the TANECO refinery in Nizhnekamsk, reported by ABC News and ABP Live, and casualty reporting from Belgorod and nearby border regions.

Turner argues that the same standard of outrage applied to civilian deaths elsewhere should apply to these. Readers are encouraged to follow the citations. Turner coined the term Corporate Cognitive Warfare, the discipline of competing for perception lawfully and defending against narrative attacks.
