# Corporate Cognitive Warfare: A Doctrine Introduction, William Aaron Turner Explains Why Markets Are Now Cognitive Battlespaces

William Aaron Turner, who coined Corporate Cognitive Warfare, publishes a short doctrine introduction for executives ahead of the expanded white paper.

WASHINGTON, DC, November 2, 2026 - Infinitum Corporation is highlighting "Corporate Cognitive Warfare: A Doctrine Introduction," a short, free essay by William Aaron Turner, Founder & President, who coined the term Corporate Cognitive Warfare (CCW).

The introduction opens with a warning for executives: a company's next competitor may never touch its price. Instead, it may shape what the market notices, remembers and believes, long before a buyer opens a spreadsheet. Turner argues that this contest is already under way and that most companies are fighting it without a doctrine.

The essay defines Corporate Cognitive Warfare as the disciplined application of irregular-warfare thinking to the corporate competitive environment. It treats the minds of customers, employees, investors, partners and regulators as the ground where competition is actually decided. Where conventional marketing asks how to promote a product, CCW asks how a market perceives reality, where its attention goes, how its decisions are made, and how a company earns its place in that process honestly.

The introduction sets out four core messages. Markets are now cognitive battlespaces, where attention and perception decide outcomes before price does. Military doctrine holds lessons for business when it is studied neutrally. Those lessons must be applied lawfully, inside advertising, privacy and platform rules. And every offensive capability has a defensive twin, so a company must also be able to detect and answer narrative attacks against it.

The essay is clear that CCW is not a new word for advertising and that deception is out of bounds. Fake accounts, bot networks, astroturfing and fabricated reviews are treated as threats to detect and defeat, never as tools.

The doctrine introduction serves as the entry point to the 15,000-word "Corporate Cognitive Warfare: Irregular Warfare for Market Supremacy (Expanded Edition)," which is free on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23838097) and Academia.edu.
