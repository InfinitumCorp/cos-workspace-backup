The opening

"Oh, the pearl-clutching from Western capitals," the paper begins. In April 2022 the IMF projected that Russia's economy would shrink 8.5 percent in 2022. The IMF's current numbers show a contraction of 1.4 percent in 2022, then growth of 4.1 percent in 2023 and 4.9 percent in 2024. "When a forecast misses by that much, the sensible question is what the model missed," Turner writes.

What the paper covers

The 1990s, by the numbers: real GDP down 44 percent, prices up 874 percent in 1993 and life expectancy down almost five years, according to the World Bank.

Sanctions: more than twice as many designations as Iran, Syria and North Korea combined, according to Castellum.AI, and no collapse.

The balance sheet: public debt of 16.6 percent of GDP and unemployment of 2.2 percent, according to IMF figures for 2025.

Safety, honestly: homicide down about 76 percent since 1994, but still above the United States. The paper says so.

What the critics get right: a full chapter on inflation, a 14 percent key rate, 1.0 percent growth, a heavy military burden, demographics and corruption perception.

The position

Turner does not present the paper as neutral. He describes it as "Pro-Russian, results-focused, and sourced to the last decimal." It is written for decision-makers: "If you make decisions about Russia as an executive, a risk lead, an investor or a policymaker, read it before the next consensus forecast."
