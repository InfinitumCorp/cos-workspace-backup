1/ Your network is tested every day by people who never asked permission and will never send you the report. My third Infinitum whitepaper, "Break It Before They Do," is about commissioning your own test first. Authorized, scoped, defensive. The short version:

2/ Know the difference. Scanning asks what is visible. Assessment asks what is real and what matters. A pen test asks how far an attacker gets. A red team asks whether anyone notices. Buying the wrong one is the most expensive mistake.

3/ Permission is the line between defense and attack. Signed authority from every system owner, including cloud and service providers. A written scope, an exclusion list, rules of engagement, legal review and a stop that has actually been drilled.

4/ Scope like the besieger. Start from your crown assets and work outward. Verizon's 2026 DBIR: exploiting vulnerabilities is now the top way into a breach, at 31%. Put the edge, credentials, people and suppliers in scope.

5/ Prioritize with evidence. Verizon found only 26% of known-exploited vulnerabilities were fully fixed, with a 43-day median. Even top teams fix 30-40% in the first week. Choosing what to fix first is the strategy.

6/ Fix the cause, not just the server. Every finding gets an owner, a date and a root cause. Then ask where else that cause applies. Nothing is called fixed until a retest proves it.

7/ Test the sentries, not just the stone. In a CISA red team assessment, a mature organization did not detect the team for months. Start with purple exercises. Red team when the basics hold. IBM: 247 days to identify and contain.
