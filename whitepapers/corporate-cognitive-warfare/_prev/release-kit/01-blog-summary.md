# Corporate Cognitive Warfare: The Expanded Edition

*Infinitum Corporation · White Paper · Expanded Edition, October 2026 · By William Aaron Turner*

Markets are contests over perception. Customers, employees, investors, regulators and the public each decide what a company is worth based on how they understand it, and that understanding is shaped every day by competitors, journalists, algorithms and, increasingly, hostile actors. **Corporate Cognitive Warfare**, a term coined by William Aaron Turner, Founder and President of Infinitum Corporation, is the discipline of competing on that terrain lawfully and defending it well.

The expanded edition grows the original paper to about 15,000 words. It keeps the original structure, definitions and six-phase framework (Understand, Shape, Seed, Amplify, Exploit, Protect) and adds four bodies of material:

- **A neutral doctrinal foundation.** The paper reviews published literature on Russian reflexive control theory (from Vladimir Lefebvre's reflexive-games work through Diane Chotikul's 1986 Naval Postgraduate School study and Timothy Thomas's analyses for the Foreign Military Studies Office) and on Chinese cognitive domain operations and the "three warfares" (drawing on Elsa Kania, Nathan Beauchamp-Mustafaga of RAND, Josh Baughman of the China Aerospace Studies Institute, and the U.S. Department of Defense's 2023 report to Congress), alongside NATO's research on cognitive warfare. Each tradition is presented analytically, with the caveats its own analysts raise. The paper takes no side.
- **Detailed corporate applications.** Guerrilla marketing, persuasion psychology ("marketing psyops"), algorithmic optimization, social influence and targeted advertising, each set inside the rules that govern it: FTC endorsement and review rules, GDPR, the Digital Services Act, CCPA/CPRA and platform policies.
- **Twelve illustrative use cases**, from a challenger reframing a category and a guerrilla product launch to countering a viral falsehood, defending against a fake-review attack, executive deepfakes, visibility in AI-generated answers and internal communication during a merger.
- **A defensive playbook**: a threat taxonomy, the DISARM shared vocabulary, narrative sensing, prebunking, decision hygiene against provocation, response tiers and regular exercises.

The paper draws a firm line. Fake accounts, bot networks, astroturfing, fabricated reviews and disinformation are treated only as threats to detect and defeat, never as tools. Every source is public and was checked before release.
