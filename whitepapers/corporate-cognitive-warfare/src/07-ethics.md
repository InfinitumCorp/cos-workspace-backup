# 7. Ethical and Legal Boundaries

Corporate Cognitive Warfare must remain within applicable laws governing advertising, competition, privacy, and securities disclosure. Techniques that involve outright fabrication of facts, coordinated inauthentic behavior that violates platform terms, or the deliberate targeting of vulnerable populations for harm fall outside legitimate practice. Organizations that sacrifice long-term trust for short-term cognitive gains typically destroy the very asset—reputation and legitimacy—that enables durable advantage.

Recommended safeguards include legal review of campaigns, transparency where required by regulation, clear internal policies distinguishing competitive framing from deception, and continuous monitoring for unintended societal effects.

## 7.1 The Regulatory Map

The table below summarizes the main rules that bound the techniques in this paper. It is a map, not legal advice. Requirements vary by jurisdiction and change over time, and campaigns should be reviewed by qualified counsel.

| Rule | What it covers | Relevance |
| FTC Act § 5; FTC Policy Statement on Deception (1983) | Deceptive acts or practices in commerce | All claims, framing and interface design |
| FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255 (revised 2023) | Endorsements, testimonials, disclosure of material connections | Creators, employees, affiliates, experts |
| FTC Rule on Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465 (effective 2024) | Fake reviews, purchased reviews, insider reviews, review suppression, fake social-media indicators | Review programs; social proof; engagement metrics |
| FTC Statement on Comparative Advertising, 16 CFR § 14.15 | Truthful comparisons naming competitors | Challenger messaging |
| Lanham Act § 43(a), 15 U.S.C. § 1125 | False or misleading representations; false association | Competitor claims; ambush marketing |
| SEC Regulation FD, 17 CFR Part 243 | Selective disclosure of material nonpublic information | Investor narratives |
| EU GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679 | Lawful basis, special categories, right to object to direct marketing | Targeting; data-driven persuasion |
| EU Digital Services Act, Regulation (EU) 2022/2065 | Ad transparency; limits on profiling-based ads | Paid media on platforms in the EU |
| EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 50 | Transparency duties, including disclosure of deepfakes | Synthetic media in campaigns |

## 7.2 Red Lines

The following practices are outside Corporate Cognitive Warfare as Infinitum defines it, whatever their short-term effect:

- Fabricating facts, data, reviews, testimonials or endorsements.
- Creating or buying fake accounts, bot networks or artificial engagement.
- Concealing a sponsor behind a "grassroots" campaign, front group or undisclosed paid voice.
- Impersonating people, organizations or official bodies, including through synthetic media.
- Spreading false or misleading claims about competitors, critics or public issues.
- Planting false information to influence search engines or AI systems.
- Designing interfaces that trick people into choices they would not otherwise make.
- Targeting children, people in crisis or people defined by sensitive characteristics with manipulative techniques.
- Selectively disclosing material nonpublic information, or planting leaks to move markets.
- Harassing, threatening or doxxing critics, journalists or competitors' staff.

## 7.3 The Ethics Test

Legal compliance is the floor. Four questions help teams decide whether a lawful tactic is also wise:

1. **Transparency:** Would we be comfortable if the audience knew exactly what we did and why?
2. **Truth:** Is every claim true and substantiated, and is the overall impression accurate?
3. **Autonomy:** Does the tactic help people decide, or does it work by stopping them from thinking?
4. **Durability:** Will this build or erode trust if we do it a hundred times?

A tactic that fails any of these questions should be redesigned or dropped.

## 7.4 Governance

Ethics needs a mechanism. Recommended governance includes a written Corporate Cognitive Warfare policy approved by leadership; a standing review group spanning marketing, communications, legal, privacy and security; documented substantiation files for claims; disclosure checklists for all partner content; a register of campaigns that use personal data, with their lawful basis; periodic audits of creator and affiliate compliance; and a confidential channel through which staff can raise concerns about tactics without fear of reprisal.
