Today I'm sharing my second Infinitum Corporation whitepaper: "Trenches and Drones: Network Defense for a War of Attrition."

The lens is irregular warfare, used as analysis only. I read public work from CSIS and the standards bodies on the modern battlefield, then asked what it teaches people who defend mid-size organizations. Depth. Cheap mass. Signals that lie. Constant observation. Supply lines that are never safe. A comparison, not a claim that your network is a front line. The paper takes no side and makes no claim about any party, and it leaves out casualty, loss and performance figures for everyone.

Then the numbers:

- Verizon's 2026 DBIR: exploiting vulnerabilities is now the top way into a breach, at 31%, up from 20%. Only 26% of known-exploited vulnerabilities in its data were fully remediated, and the median time to patch rose to 43 days.

1/ Nobody wins this fight in a day. My second Infinitum whitepaper, "Trenches and Drones: Network Defense for a War of Attrition," treats security as a contest of cost and endurance. It takes no side. Five fronts, seven moves. The short version:

2/ Trenches. Zero trust guidance says the implicit trust zone "must be as small as possible." Assume the first line is crossed. Segment, limit privilege, and rehearse recovery. Depth makes every step cost more.

3/ Drones. Verizon's 2026 DBIR: exploiting vulnerabilities is now the top way into a breach, 31%, up from 20%. Median time to patch rose to 43 days. Answer cheap, constant pressure with cheap, constant controls: MFA, edge patching, flood protection.

4/ Electronic warfare. Aviation data cited by EASA and IATA show GPS signal loss events up 220% from 2021 to 2024. Signals get jammed and spoofed. Keep a second source for time, identity and voice. Verify out of band.

5/ Sensors. You are being watched. Know your outside-in signature, then build detection around decisions. IBM: mean time to identify and contain a breach was 247 days. Information is cheap. Sense-making is the hard part.

6/ Supply lines. Verizon: third parties were involved in 48% of breaches, up 60%. Tier your suppliers. Keep exports and manual fallbacks. Rehearse the day one of them goes dark.

7/ Command and people. Pre-authorize actions. Name deputies. Keep a channel that doesn't depend on what you're defending. ISC2: 67% report a staffing shortage. Endurance is the strategy. Rotate, cross-train, keep a bench.
