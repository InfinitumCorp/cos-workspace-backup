
New from Infinitum Corporation: my second whitepaper, "Trenches and Drones: Network Defense for a War of Attrition."

The idea: attrition is a contest of cost and endurance. Your adversaries don't need to beat you once. They need to keep the pressure on until something gives.

Public analysis of the modern battlefield describes depth, cheap mass, jammed and spoofed signals, constant observation and fragile supply lines. My shorthand: the grind of the trenches fused with science fiction. A comparison, not a claim. The paper takes no side and makes no claim about any party.

Five fronts, translated for a mid-size network:
- Trenches: depth beats a hard shell.
- Drones: answer cheap mass with cheap layers.
- Electronic warfare: keep a second source for time, identity and voice.
- Sensors: know what the outside can see.
- Supply lines: your vendors are your rear area.

Plus two cross-cutting concerns, command and people. Inside: seven moves, a 30/60/90-day plan, a 12-month roadmap, a 30-item self-assessment and three hypothetical scenarios.
