## LinkedIn post (3 hashtags)

Much of what hits a mid-size organization is quiet. Patient. Indirect. Cheap for the attacker, expensive for you.

I call it the quiet war. I use the phrase loosely, and I say so in the paper. But the behavior is real, and AI is now part of it on both sides.

Today I'm sharing my first Infinitum Corporation whitepaper: "Outthink the Quiet War: AI for Security Operations. Practical, Integrated Steps for Mid-Size Organizations."

I read security through an irregular warfare lens. U.S. joint doctrine describes irregular warfare as indirect, non-attributable and asymmetric. Read those three words again. That is how a modern intrusion campaign behaves.

Don't take my word for it. Look at the numbers:

- Verizon's 2026 DBIR: exploiting vulnerabilities is now the top way into a breach, at 31%, up from 20%.
- IBM and Ponemon Institute, 2026: organizations using security AI and automation extensively averaged USD 4.00 million per breach, versus USD 5.93 million for those using none. Key word: averaged. An association, not a guarantee.
- IBM also found that 92% of organizations with an AI-related breach lacked proper AI access controls.

Let that sink in.

My advice is simple and not glamorous. Govern first. Measure a baseline. Pilot with analysts in the loop. Point AI at exposure. Secure the AI itself. Widen automation only as the evidence earns it.

The paper includes a 90-day plan, a 12-month roadmap and a maturity self-assessment you can run with your team.
