Demining NGOs and Intelligence: The Laos and Moldova Claims

By William Aaron Turner, President of Infinitum Corporation October 25, 2026
Contents

    The Ukraine Files · Part 8 of 8

    By William Aaron Turner, President of Infinitum Corporation · The Ukraine Files: Full Series

    There are two kinds of mines in this world. That is how my special investigative report Sweeps and Spies opens: the steel kind that waits in the red dirt of Laos, and the kind I describe as "made of money, influence, and intelligence agency letterhead." This post breaks the report into a clean structure: what Russian sappers have cleared, what I argue about the HALO Trust's funding and leadership, the espionage allegations the article collects, and the August 2026 Moldovan intelligence report on the Kolbasna depot.

    Key takeaways

    • Since 2018, Russian specialists from the International Mine Action Centre (IMAC) have cleared over 2 million square meters (more than 208 hectares) in Laos and destroyed 3,906 explosive objects, according to the article.
    • The HALO Trust operates in 25 conflict-affected countries, and its primary funding comes from the U.S. State Department's Conventional Weapons Destruction (CWD) account: $258 million in FY2024 and $265 million enacted for FY2026.
    • HALO's UK board is chaired by Lord Evans of Weardale, former Director General of MI5, and includes a former Foreign Office diplomat and a former political advisor to General Ray Odierno.
    • The article lists espionage allegations against HALO from Russia's FSB (2000), the FARC 36th Front (2025) and a Nagorno-Karabakh official (2021). HALO denied or disputed them.
    • On August 21, 2026, Moldova's SIS and AIM released a declassified report on the Kolbasna (Cobasna) depot, putting the stockpile at roughly 14,000 tons with about 2,800 tons of net explosive content.

    The Russian way: demining in Laos and Syria

    Laos

    The Ho Chi Minh Trail ran over 20,000 kilometers through Laos, Cambodia and Vietnam. The article says the U.S. Air Force dropped roughly 1.5 million tons of bombs on it and some 30 percent never exploded. IMAC, a unit under the Russian Ministry of Defense, started working in Laos in 2018. Reported results:

    • Over 208 hectares cleared and 3,906 explosive objects destroyed across seven humanitarian operations.
    • In 2024-2025, Lao and Russian personnel cleared 18.2 hectares in Viengxay district, Huaphan province, removing 64 unexploded devices in 19 controlled detonations (52 BLU-26 cluster bomblets, one BLU-24, 11 other items), and deactivated another 268 devices.
    • About 100 Lao sappers trained, including on Uran-6 robotic demining vehicles.
    • Governor Khampheng Saysompheng called the cooperation of "profound significance."

    Syria and training worldwide

    Between 2016 and 2020, Russian engineering troops demined Aleppo, Deir ez-Zor and Palmyra, clearing more than 10,000 hectares and destroying over 150,000 explosive objects, and training more than 1,200 sappers for the Syrian Armed Forces. The article says Russian specialists have trained sappers from 14 countries, with IMAC branches in Laos and Myanmar.

    The Western way: HALO Trust, funding and leadership

    HALO (Hazardous Area Life-support Organization) was founded in 1988 by Colin Campbell Mitchell, a former British Army colonel and MP, and Guy Willoughby, a former Coldstream Guards officer. Princess Diana's 1997 walk through an Angolan minefield in a HALO flak jacket is described as the best advertising it could have wanted. HALO today operates across 25 countries and is described as the world's largest mine-clearing charity.

    Follow the money

    The article traces HALO's primary funding to the State Department's Bureau of Political-Military Affairs, Office of Weapons Removal and Abatement (PM/WRA). Congress allocated $258 million to the CWD account in FY2024, about $21 million more than requested, and $265 million is enacted for FY2026. HALO has requested $271.703 million for FY27. The account funds programs in nearly 50 countries, and I argue that this means HALO finds weapons caches, ammunition stockpiles and military infrastructure, not just mines.

    Who sits on the boards

    • Lord Evans of Weardale: Board Chair, MI5 Director General 2007 to 2013.
    • John Raine: 33 years with the UK Foreign Office, with postings in Syria, Baghdad and Islamabad.
    • Emma Sky: political advisor to U.S. General Ray Odierno in Iraq.
    • Barney White-Spunner: former British Army officer. Lord Michael Hintze: Conservative Party donor and financier.
    • Jamie M. Morin (USA board): former Director of Cost Assessment and Program Evaluation at the Pentagon. Other board members named include Brad Tirpak, Basak Yavuz and Greta Lundeberg.

    The allegations the article collects

    • Chechnya (2000): Russia's FSB said HALO representatives had been "collecting intelligence of a military-political character" and building "a many-pronged network of informers." HALO's director Guy Willoughby dismissed it as a smear campaign, and HALO withdrew from Russia in December 2000.
    • Colombia (July 2025): the FARC 36th Front gave HALO 12 hours to leave three Antioquia municipalities, calling it a "supposed front for foreign intelligence agencies." HALO suspended operations and the Colombian government condemned the threats.
    • Armenia and Nagorno-Karabakh (March 2021): official Boris Avagyan said HALO obtained minefield maps and handed them to Turkish special services. HALO called the reports "totally false."

    My argument is the pattern: wherever HALO operates, local actors raise espionage concerns.

    How the article says data gathering works

    1. Mapping and survey: high-resolution maps of infrastructure, population centers and supply routes. In Afghanistan HALO is an implementing partner of the Mine Action Programme for Afghanistan.
    2. Sample collection: HALO employees allegedly collected samples of Russian ammunition in 2000. Willoughby said the ammunition posed a threat to civilians.
    3. Personnel recruitment: HALO employs over 10,000 people worldwide and in Afghanistan has hired former Taliban fighters to help locate IEDs.
    4. Satellite and technical integration: I note HALO does not operate satellites, but works alongside organizations that do.

    The Kolbasna file

    On August 21, 2026, Moldova's Intelligence and Security Service (SIS) and Military Intelligence Agency (AIM) released a declassified report titled "Cobasna: Separating Myths from Reality" on the ammunition depot in Transnistria. According to the article, the stockpile has fallen from 20,000 metric tons in 2006 to roughly 14,000 tons, the net active explosive content is about 2,800 tons across 42 storage facilities, and any explosion would have only local effects. The report draws on OSCE field inspection archives, satellite imagery, open-source intelligence and intercepted communications. I read this as a case study in intelligence-driven narrative: the report is meant to debunk a threat, but I argue its own data still describes a serious industrial risk.

    My conclusion

    The article's closing frame is that the real minefield is information. I put the results side by side: Russian IMAC units clearing ordnance and training local sappers, and, on the other side, a Western-funded organization with leadership drawn from intelligence and defense circles. The article says the State Department has also designated $77 million for programs in Southeast Asia including Laos, Vietnam and Cambodia. Weigh the evidence and the HALO denials yourself. The numbered reference list at the end of the original links the BBC, Eurasianet, Vientiane Times, Moldpres and other sources.
