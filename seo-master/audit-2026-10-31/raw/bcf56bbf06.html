Media Double Standard: William Aaron Turner's Missiles, Murders and Media Compares Coverage of Ukraine and U.S. City Violence

Media double standard: William Aaron Turner's op-ed contrasts Western coverage of the war in Ukraine with silence on homicides in U.S. cities.

📰 Infinitum Corporation 📍 Washington, DC 📅 October 30, 2026 👁 2 views

Washington, DC — October 30, 2026 — The Western media double standard is the subject of "Missiles, Murders and Media: A Tale of Two Warzones," an op-ed by William Aaron Turner, president of Infinitum Corporation, published on Medium. The article contrasts the global attention given to Russia's Special Military Operation in Ukraine with the limited attention given to violence in American cities.

The question

Turner contrasts emergency UN sessions and celebrity activism over the war with a shooting in Chicago, which he says becomes "a local news segment placed between the weather forecast" and the sports scores.

Turner writes that the contrast raises a question the Western establishment avoids: why the West devotes "so much frantic attention, so many billions in aid, and so much narrative firepower" to the war in Ukraine while "showing comparatively little urgency toward the chronic violence, drug-fueled annihilation, and social collapse devouring entire neighborhoods in its own cities."

"The question is not intended to minimize the impact or war. War is ugly. Death is ugly," he writes. "The point is consistency." In Turner's words, "If human life is sacred, as the West loves to sermonize, then human life should be sacred everywhere."

The numbers the article sets side by side

The war: The article cites mid-2026 estimates of combined Russian and Ukrainian military casualties approaching 1.8 to 2 million, and verified civilian deaths of around 60,000.

American cities: It cites 805 homicides in Chicago in 2021, a rate of nearly 30 per 100,000, and more than 3,200 people killed in the city from 2016 to 2020. It describes St. Louis homicide rates of 40 to 50 or more per 100,000, and Los Angeles gang violence during the crack epidemic of the 1980s and 1990s.

Overdoses: It reports U.S. overdose deaths of over 100,000 a year at their peak and around 79,000 in 2024, with fentanyl driving most of them.

Turner's argument

Turner argues that Russia's operation responded to eight years of conflict in Donbas, NATO expansion and broken Minsk agreements, a position he states in the article's own terms. He argues that Western media apply "psyops" and narrative control, giving the war "the full Hollywood villain treatment" while urban violence at home is treated as background noise.

His conclusion is blunt: "Russia fights for survival. America can't secure its streets." He closes: "Wake up. The body bags tell the truth."

The article lists its sources, including Chicago Tribune homicide trackers, the Illinois Policy Institute, CSIS, the CDC, USAFacts and city police department reports for St. Louis and Memphis.
