This handbook examines how low-cost sensors, including first-person-view (FPV) drones, commercial satellite imagery and the phones of ordinary people, have changed surveillance and reconnaissance in contemporary conflict. It presents a conceptual framework for fusing three source types, drone observation, social media intelligence (SOCMINT) and open-source intelligence (OSINT), into a continuously updated common operating picture for decision-makers. The text is organized around six governing principles and a five-phase collection and analysis cycle with a feedback loop, and it describes the roles of each source type at a general level. Further chapters address protecting one's own operations and personnel, the composition and training of multidisciplinary teams, and ethical and legal boundaries, including sovereignty and airspace law, privacy and the need to corroborate sources. A chapter on future trends discusses autonomy, generative tools and cryptographic verification of content. The handbook ends with summary principles, a glossary and references to open data sources and standards. It is an author's synthesis rather than official doctrine and does not report independent evaluation of the framework.
