This study examines decentralized disinformation, which it contrasts with traditional centralized propaganda: rather than originating messages, influence actors attach themselves to narratives that already have organic traction and spread them across platforms and audiences. The author introduces an actor-affect-affordance model and describes, analytically, how seed content is repackaged, how narratives evolve, and why two practices it names content shadowing, the blending of falsehood with verifiable fact, and content clipping, the extraction of statements from their context, gain reach, including through monetized clip channels. A chapter on leaderless protest movements discusses how the same dynamics can mobilize genuine grievances and leave movements open to hijacking. Case studies include the 2024 Southport riots in England and the 2024 Gen Z protests in Kenya. Further chapters discuss the roles of major platforms, the psychology of outrage and sharing, and defensive measures for platforms and other actors, followed by a conclusion and key takeaways. The text states that it is educational in purpose and intended to support awareness and effective defenses rather than alarm.
