This twelve-page guide examines the digital exposure of military personnel and argues that the modern battlefield is defined not only by terrain but by visibility in the electromagnetic spectrum and online. Drawing on quoted statements by U.S. Army commanders and publicly reported examples, its introduction describes how smartphones, fitness trackers and social media posts can reveal locations and patterns of life. Six short chapters then address signal-blocking (Faraday) technology, hidden metadata in files and images, the collection of personal data about service members and their families through social media and data brokers, location tracking, routine cyber hygiene as defined by the U.S. Department of Defense, and categories of protective digital tools organized by personnel risk level. The conclusion frames reducing one's digital signature, described as blending into the noise rather than becoming invisible, as a condition of survival. The guide is written as a concise field-manual-style awareness text for individual personnel and units. It is an author's synthesis, not official doctrine, and does not report independent testing of the practices or tools it discusses.
