Markets are contests over perception.

Customers, employees, investors, regulators and the public decide what a company is worth based on how they understand it. That understanding is shaped every day by competitors, journalists, ranking systems and, sometimes, actors who do not follow the rules. **Corporate Cognitive Warfare™**, a term coined in 2026 by William Aaron Turner, Founder and President of Infinitum Corporation®, names the discipline of competing on that terrain lawfully—and defending it when someone else will not.

The expanded edition keeps the original structure, definitions and six-phase framework—Understand, Shape, Seed, Amplify, Exploit, Protect—and adds four bodies of material. First, a **neutral doctrinal foundation** drawn from published literature: reflexive control theory in Soviet and Russian writing, Chinese discussions of the “three warfares” and cognitive domain operations, and NATO research that treats cognitive warfare as a study area with resilience as a primary response. Traditions differ in vocabulary and purpose. The paper presents them analytically, takes no side, and deliberately omits campaign attributions. Corporate applications are **not** battlefield validation.

Second, **lawful corporate applications** for five technique families: guerrilla marketing, ethical persuasion psychology, algorithmic optimization (legitimate SEO/GEO), social influence and targeted advertising. Each sits inside the rules that bound it—FTC endorsement and review rules, GDPR, the Digital Services Act, CCPA/CPRA and platform policies. “Low-signature” means quiet, efficient and well-targeted. It never means hiding who is speaking when the law or platform rules require disclosure.

Third, **twelve illustrative use cases**—composites, not client case studies—from a challenger reframing a category to defending against fake-review attacks or an executive deepfake. Fourth, a **defensive playbook**: narrative sensing, prebunking, decision hygiene, response tiers and exercises. Fake accounts, bot networks, astroturfing, fabricated reviews and disinformation are treated only as threats to detect and defeat, never as tools.


What Corporate Cognitive Warfare™ is not matters as much as what it is. It is not disinformation, not coordinated inauthentic behavior, not covert sponsorship where disclosure is required, and not exploitation of vulnerable audiences. It is a structured way to understand cognitive terrain, frame choices truthfully, win attention through legitimate means, build credible advocates, and defend reputation and decision-making against hostile influence. Trust is treated as the center of gravity: in a repeated market game it is the advantage competitors cannot copy quickly.

Key takeaways stay close to the source. Attention, trust and the framing of choices often decide outcomes before price or features are compared. Military doctrine offers a disciplined vocabulary for that terrain; in commerce the advantage is durable only when earned with truthful claims, disclosed relationships, authentic engagement and respect for privacy. Every organization is already a target for hostile influence, so cognitive defense—monitoring, prebunking, accurate response, decision hygiene—is basic organizational hygiene. Asymmetry favors the side that understands the audience and moves fastest within the law, not merely the side that spends most.

This Medium piece summarizes the expanded whitepaper. It does not expand operational recommendations beyond the source, and it does not claim deployments, customers or validated combat use. Read the full paper for the framework, use cases, ethics boundaries and references.
