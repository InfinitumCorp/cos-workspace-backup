This handbook addresses the challenge that inexpensive, commercially available drones pose to ground forces, which its foreword describes as a change in the character of modern warfare. The author states that it consolidates lessons from reporting on the war in Ukraine, NATO exercises and U.S. doctrinal publications, and it is addressed to soldiers and small-unit leaders. The text is organized around a five-step counter-unmanned aircraft system (C-UAS) framework and a layered-defense concept. It surveys, at the level of categories, detection systems, electronic warfare and its stated limits, kinetic interception, the particular problem of fiber-optic-tethered drones, signature reduction and thermal concealment, and the integration of these measures across unit echelons. It closes with summary principles, a glossary and a list of references that includes U.S. Army and Joint Interagency Task Force 401 publications. Several product and performance statements are attributed to manufacturers or are noted by the text as awaiting independent verification. The handbook is an author's synthesis of public sources rather than official doctrine, and it does not present original testing or independent battlefield validation.
